Built, not offset

The four lines are not offsets of one another. Each is constructed from its own stem width, so the terminals keep their proportions instead of collapsing to spikes as the axis lightens.

Contrast rides the axis

Thin over stem runs 0.49 at Black and 0.35 at Regular: horizontals lose weight faster than stems, so arches, bars and bowl tops keep drawing as the stems go to wire. Without it the light end reads as a geometric sans.

Still to fix

The g is the only letter still offset from a traced master, and it narrows across the ladder while everything beside it holds width — visible as a gap opening after the r at Regular. It wants rebuilding on the same parts as the rest.

04 · character set

Three parts, most of the alphabet

The h broke into a cut terminal, a rounded-square foot and a shoulder. n is the h without its ascender; b closes the shoulder at the baseline; m repeats it on a narrower width; u is symmetric rather than a flipped n; w overlaps two of the u's bowls. p is the b turned over and q is that turned again, so all four share a point order.

The ring, from your artwork

The o you sent is not an ellipse: its outer walls run dead straight for the middle third of the height, and the counter is a rounded rectangle with one radius on all four corners. o, 0, c and e are all built on that shape. The counter's corners are radiused off its width — off its height they went square at the light end and drove into the cap, leaving a wall of ten units where the shoulder should be thickest.

Where the cut changes

One angle carries the family: every cut terminal climbs 0.34 to the right. The r's arm takes it turned over. Three places refuse it — the f's hook, the k's arm and leg, and the tails of j, y and t — because the stroke there is horizontal or already climbing at 45°, and a slanted cut on top of that reads as a slip. The s refuses all of it: with no straight edge and no extremum to hang a point on, it is drawn as a centreline with a width rule rather than as an outline.

The m ends on a stem and the a opens on a bowl, so the two edges never face each other: the stem holds its full height while the a's left edge falls away above and below its widest point. Equal sidebearings put a hole there. Bearings are now set by what the edge does — flat takes the full 47 units, round 0.80 of it, an open or diagonal side 0.62 — and the pair takes a further 24 units on top.

07 · edit the outlines yourself
out as SVG, back as SVG

Every glyph comes out as one plain path on a font grid — em box, baseline, x-height and descender ruled in pink, the glyph named on the cell. Coordinates are already in SVG's downward axis, so nothing carries a transform and nothing gets baked when it returns. Redraw whatever is wrong, drop the file back in the chat, and I will read the paths straight in as masters.

the two masters are what the axis interpolates between — edit both if you want the change to hold across every weight

Four rules for the round trip
  1. Keep each path's id — that is how I know which glyph it is.
  2. Keep each glyph inside its own cell. Move it within the cell and you have changed its sidebearings, which is fine and I will read them.
  3. Flatten or expand before saving, so the path data is absolute and carries no transform.
  4. Save as plain SVG, not Illustrator or Figma native. Ignore the #guides layer.

Anything you hand back stops being parametric and becomes a fixed master, so the g is the model: it interpolates by offset rather than by rule. Send both weights where you can. Still missing from the set: the ampersand, the numbers (pulled out until they are drawn), and every capital but B — the ampersand because its stroke crosses itself, and these outlines fill even-odd, so a crossing punches a hole instead of joining. It wants drawing as a silhouette.
